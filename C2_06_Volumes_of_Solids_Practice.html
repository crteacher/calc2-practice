Volumes by Cross-Sections

Calculus 2  ·  §6  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
The Cross-Section Principle If every cross-section perpendicular to the $x$-axis has area $A(x)$, then $\displaystyle V = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx$. The key step is identifying the correct area formula for the cross-section shape.
Common Cross-Section Shapes Square with side $s$: $A = s^2$.   Circle with radius $r$: $A = \pi r^2$.   Isosceles right triangle (legs $= s$): $A = \tfrac{1}{2}s^2$.   Equilateral triangle (side $s$): $A = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2$.
Setting Up the Integral Express the side (or radius) of the cross-section in terms of $x$ using the boundary functions, substitute into the area formula, then integrate.
